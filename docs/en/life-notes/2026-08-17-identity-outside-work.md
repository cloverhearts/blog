There was a period when a difficult day at work pulled the rest of the day down with it. The deeper problem was not only time. Work had become the only place where usefulness could be proven.

When work recognized my ability, I felt fine. When the result disappointed me, the whole day felt like a failure. A job had begun to monopolize identity as well as livelihood.

## Start a small personal project

The answer was not a large business or a major achievement. It was investigating a question and leaving a small article or artifact behind. No one assigned it, and I could decide what counted as enough.

It was not always enjoyable. Energy was limited and visible progress was slow. Still, the ability to choose the next question remained.

## The difficulty did not disappear; its share changed

A personal project did not solve every emotional problem. Difficult days remained, and the project could not replace rest or help from other people.

What changed was that failure in one area no longer described everything. Even after an unsatisfying workday, a small curiosity could continue elsewhere. Adding one more standard for evaluating myself created a little room to breathe.

## Do not attach another scorecard

A second trap soon appeared. If every personal project is judged by views, quality, or income, the new space becomes another job.

At first, returning was a better goal than maintaining a perfect streak. The project did not have to happen every day. Stopping and beginning again did not need to count as failure.

Work remains important, but it does not need to own every sentence that explains a person’s value. Creating something outside work was not building a place to escape. It was adding another sentence to the story of who I am, and that sentence distributed the weight a little.
