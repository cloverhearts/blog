I once read a web novel written by someone close to me and noticed several weaknesses. I could explain where the flow broke and which explanations repeated.

I did not say everything immediately. The observations were not necessarily wrong; the problem was that accurate feedback is not always useful feedback.

## Accuracy judges only the content

Accuracy asks whether a critique identifies a real problem. Useful feedback asks additional questions: Is the person ready to hear it? Can they continue writing afterward? Which change should come first?

Ten criticisms may all be correct. If the only conclusion left with a new writer is “I have no talent,” the feedback was accurate without being helpful.

## Feedback needs an order

Holding back does not mean offering only praise. It means selecting the one or two points that help the person continue.

1. Ask whether the person wants a reaction or an edit.
2. Identify what should remain.
3. Choose one or two high-impact problems.
4. Discuss a concrete part of the work, not the person’s ability.
5. Add a small suggestion that can be tested in the next attempt.

## Not speaking immediately can also be a choice

There is no duty to deliver every observation as soon as it appears. A reader can share enjoyment first and offer detailed comments when the writer is ready to revise.

Silence should not become an excuse for avoiding uncomfortable truth. Honest evaluation is necessary when it is requested or when an important decision depends on it. Even then, accuracy and delivery remain separate questions.

The purpose of advice is not to prove that the adviser found the problem. It is to help the other person write the next chapter.
