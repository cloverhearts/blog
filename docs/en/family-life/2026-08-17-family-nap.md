There was no special schedule that day. We were not celebrating anything or returning from a trip. A family of four lay down in one bed and took a nap.

It was too small to call an event. That may be why I wanted to remember it.

## I thought memory needed a plot

When recording a day, it is easy to search for an event: a new place, an achievement, or an unexpected problem. A day without one can pass with only a date attached.

But the moments we later want to revisit may not be dramatic. The cramped feeling of one bed, waking when someone moved, and closing my eyes again while everyone remained nearby can become memories without a plot.

## Explanations arrive after happy moments

At the time, no one announced that the moment was happy. There was no plan to photograph it or make it meaningful. We were tired, lay down, and fell asleep.

Only later did my attention return to the scene. Happiness does not always arrive as a vivid emotion. Sometimes it remains as a state in which people could simply stay together without concern.

## Something that will become difficult

As the children grow, four people will no longer fit naturally in one bed. Bodies become larger, schedules separate, and rooms become individual.

That changes the standard for what is worth recording. A diary can include not only what was accomplished, but also who was present when nothing happened.

There is no lesson or twist in this nap. Four people slept and woke up. It may be exactly the kind of day I will want to see again later.
